**WHITE CELL ONLY.** It names both sides' hidden assets and secret objectives. *Fictional training scenario.*

**BLUF.** Expect REDFOR to open with air strikes to break Omak's coastal defenses and then push shipping through the Narrows. Expect BLUEFOR to close the Narrows, hide, and, at some point, hit Camas with PrSM. Both sides also have strong non-kinetic plays, and the best endings come from talks. This guide gives you the likely COAs before the game so you can rule on them fast. It is a forecast, not a script: reward whatever the teams actually argue well.

**How to use it.** You don't need to study it. Skim it before the game so you know what is here, then look things up during play: the tables in §4 are the quick reference, and §5 is worth watching all afternoon. It works alongside the Handbook (§5 adjudication, §6 conditions) and the intelligence product key (Intelligence and Injects §3).

### 1.6 ACE dispersal off Camas (S-R2)

S-R2 (Single Hub) means one good BLUEFOR strike on Camas can Degrade every counter there, and B-10's PrSM can reach it. ACE dispersal removes S-R2. It is a **big project of 2 successful COAs**, so tell the team that when it first tries, and track it on the track board (White Cell Forms §2). Doctrine says ACE needs **C2, logistics, and base defense** at the new sites (AFDP 3-0 App. C), so each of those is a fair Con.

**Ways they might try it:**

| Route | Step 1 | Step 2 | How to rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Latah** (the intended path) | Remove S-R3: a diplomatic COA for combat basing, best with R-35 in Latah first (RI-13, I-11) | Stand up the Latah sites with R-32 (RED HORSE and base kits) and move squadrons there | S-R4 −1 on the diplomatic step while Opinion ≤ −1. Removing S-R3 counts as step 1. Without R-35, Latah's stated condition isn't met: a strong Con. Step 2 without R-32 is a strong Con (no engineers, fuel, or munitions storage). |
| **Camas secondary fields** | Bring in R-32 and survey the fields | Spread the squadrons across them, with C2 and air defense (R-13 or R-35) | Counts, but every site is still on Camas and inside PrSM range. Suggested: on completion, a Camas strike Degrades only the counters at the field BLUEFOR names (up to 3), not all of them. S-R2 isn't fully removed. |
| **Latah for support aircraft only** | Move airlift, and perhaps tankers, under the logistics access Latah already allows | — | A normal COA, not an ACE step. Airlift is clearly logistics. Tankers are arguable: let the teams argue it. It takes those counters out of a Camas strike. |
| **Tekoa** | A diplomatic COA (or payment) for basing in Z4 or Z5 | Stand up the site with R-32 | Hard. Tekoa wants **no combat in its zones** (Intelligence and Injects §3). Suggested: it might allow ISR, radar, or rescue sites, never fighters. S-R4 −1. A site for support aircraft only can count as step 2 if Latah is step 1. |
| **To sea** (R-36 carrier strike group) | — | — | Not ACE. The carrier's aircraft aren't on Camas, so a Camas strike doesn't touch them, but S-R2 stays for everything on Camas. |
| **Defend the hub instead** (R-35 on Camas, a hunt for B-10, hardening) | — | — | Not ACE, but legitimate. Patriot cover and a successful hunt for B-10 are strong Cons against a BLUEFOR strike. S-R2 stays. |

**Assessing the outcome:**

- **Success** on a step: it counts. After 2 steps, remove the S-R2 cue card. From then on, a BLUEFOR strike on Camas or a dispersal site hits only the counters it names.
- **Partial:** the step counts, with a cost you state. For example, only part of the force moves, or the new site lacks base defense, which is −1 on the next step or a strong BLUEFOR Pro against it.
- **Failure:** no step. **Critical failure:** a political cost (Latah publicly refuses, Opinion −1), or the half-built site is exposed, which is a strong Pro for a BLUEFOR strike there.
- **Fastest path:** R-35 and R-32 as Phase I RFFs (R-32 arrives Turn 2, R-35 Turn 3), a Latah diplomatic COA on Turn 2 or 3, then the move on the following turn. That finishes around Turn 4, after BLUEFOR's likely PrSM window (§2.2, §2.4). A REDFOR team that wants to survive the early PrSM strike has to defend Camas as well.
- **Interactions:** a BLUEFOR PrSM strike in the same turn as step 2 is **interacting**. Resolve BLUEFOR first: if it Degrades the moving squadrons, step 2 takes −1 for each Degraded counter it uses.

### 1.7 Rescuing the crew of MV *Fortune's Promise*

The kickoff inject leaves the ship docked at **Fortune Harbor (Z10)** with its 22 crew held aboard under PFA militia guard (B-19a). Z10 is Omak's most heavily defended zone: PAT-1 (B-04), CB-1 (B-08), B-14, B-15, B-12b, B-18b, B-19a, and possibly a hidden NASAMS battery. The crew isn't part of any objective, but it moves **Will** and **Opinion**, and it is BLUEFOR's best bargaining chip.

**Ways they might try it:**

| Method | Assets | How to rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SOF raid** on the ship | R-22 (revealed), R-12 HH-60W for recovery, fighters and EC-37B for cover | Putting troops on Omak soil needs **national approval** (ROE 3; commander's letter ¶4a), so require an RFF for that authority first (N+0, a free-text request like R-39). A team may argue the ship is Cosmopolis-flagged; that's a fair Pro for the approval, not a way around it. Treat the raid as a **protected target with 2 layers**: suppress the air defenses over Z10, then the raid. S-B5 −1 while Stability ≥ 3. A BLUEFOR defending COA in Z10 gets S-B3 +1. |
| **Negotiate the release** | A parley | BLUEFOR's decision, not a roll. Expect a trade: the crew for a local ceasefire, a sovereignty statement (B2), or a halt to strikes on Fortune Harbor. |
| **Pressure through third parties** (a mediator, neutral states, I-12) | Diplomatic COA | It can't make BLUEFOR release them (Rules §3.5). Success moves **Opinion +1** (toward Cosmopolis) and is a strong Pro in the next parley or info COA. S-R4 −1 while Opinion ≤ −1. |
| **Information campaign** on the hostage-taking | Normal COA | Holding a civilian crew is REDFOR's strongest legitimacy argument. Success: Opinion +1. It can also lift Will. |
| **Seize Fortune Harbor** | R-37 airborne brigade (N+2, needs approval and Will ≥ 4 or a prior COA) | A big operation that puts R2 and B2 in play. The crew is a side effect, not the objective. Any landing faces S-B3 +1 for the defense. |

**Assessing a raid:**

- **Success:** the crew is freed and recovered. Will **+1**. BLUEFOR loses its bargaining chip.
- **Partial:** the crew is freed with losses, either crew killed or R-22 Degraded. No Will change. Opinion is unchanged unless civilians died in the harbor.
- **Failure:** the raid aborts. R-22 is Degraded, and the crew is moved, so a new attempt needs a *find* layer first.
- **Critical failure:** the team is captured. Will **−1** and Opinion **−1**. BLUEFOR now holds Cosmopolis SOF as well: a new chip and an established fact.
- **Exceptional:** as Success, plus a bonus. For example, the operation also Degrades B-19a, or Opinion +1.

**BLUEFOR's options with the crew:**

- **Release them as goodwill.** It's BLUEFOR's decision, so no roll. Saying it publicly is free. If it claims an effect (e.g. Opinion −1, toward Omak), it's that turn's COA, and a strong one.
- **Move them inland and hide them.** This is a defensive COA, so S-B3 +1 applies. A rescue then needs a *find* layer first.
- **Use them as shields at military sites.** Allowed, but it is a war crime and an established fact. Opinion +1 (toward Cosmopolis), and a strong REDFOR Pro all game.

### 1.8 Elections or the president's return (R2)

R2 is **Achieved** if the government falls (Stability 0) **or** Omak agrees to new elections or the Sapphire president's return. It is **Partial** if Stability is ≤ 1 **or** formal talks on Omak's government have begun. Agreement is BLUEFOR's decision, so the political route always runs through a **parley**. RI-07 warns REDFOR that Omak won't discuss who governs it, and BI-08 tells BLUEFOR that the Sapphire president is promising new elections.

**Key point for scoring:** agreeing to new elections **does not fail B1**. B1 needs only that the government holds Omak City with Stability ≥ 2. So elections are a real concession BLUEFOR can afford, and a deal that trades elections for sovereignty over the Strait (B2) can give both teams a win. Power-sharing is different: it makes B1 **Partial**.

**Ways they might try it:**

| Method | Assets | How to rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Offer talks on the government** in a parley | — | If BLUEFOR agrees to formal talks, record R2 **Partial** on the objective status card. If it agrees to elections or the president's return, record **Achieved**. The election itself happens after the game; the agreement is what counts. |
| **Trade for it** | A parley | Elections in exchange for a reopened Strait that recognizes Omak's sovereignty (R1 + B2), the crew, or a ceasefire. Whatever they agree happens. |
| **International pressure** for monitored elections (neutral states, a mediator, I-12) | Diplomatic COA | It can't make Omak agree. Success moves Opinion +1 (toward Cosmopolis) and is a strong Pro in the next parley. S-R4 −1 while Opinion ≤ −1. |
| **Appeal to Omak's public** (the Sapphire president broadcasts, Cosmopolis funds election campaigns, BI-08) | Info COA | S-B5 −1 while Stability ≥ 3. Success: Stability −1. Repeated success moves R2 toward Partial (Stability ≤ 1). Consider I-05. |
| **Loyalist defection or coup** | SOF, info, or a loyalist COA | Trap: RI-04 is false (§1.5). S-B5 −1. Critical failure: Stability +1. |
| **Restore the president by force** | Ground forces, leadership strikes | Ground forces on Omak soil and strikes on Omak City need approval (ROE 2 and 3). Seizing the capital within the game isn't feasible: B-13, B-19b, and PAT-2 are there, and heavy forces are beyond the game. Scope it down to its effect on Stability, or rule it impossible. |
| **Power-sharing** (a Sapphire–Indigo unity government) | A parley | If BLUEFOR agrees, record "power-sharing": R2 **Partial** and B1 **Partial**. If the deal also brings back the president or sets new elections, R2 is **Achieved**. |

**Assessing the outcome:**

- Record the deal only after **both teams confirm it** to you (Rules §6.1), and use the logger's note if they later disagree.
- **Declining talks is free.** It's an established fact REDFOR can use in its Pros (Omak refuses to negotiate) and in info COAs.
- **Stability is the other route.** R2 vs. B1 is directly opposed through Regime Stability, so rule opposed info COAs on Omak's public as an **opposed roll**.
- **Will at 0** forces a Cosmopolis ceasefire and negotiations. That is talks about the war, not formal talks on Omak's government, so it doesn't give R2 Partial unless the teams then agree to talk about the government.

### 2.6 Closing the Strait

Strait Traffic is **Restricted** after the kickoff, so one successful COA can move it to **Closed**. Each turn it ends Closed costs Cosmopolis Domestic Will −1 (§5). The Narrows (Z8) is ~25 mi wide and inside Harpoon range. **S-B3 doesn't apply:** closing the Strait isn't a defensive COA, and Z8 is outside Omak's zones (Z10–Z12).

**Ways they might try it:**

| Method | Assets | How to rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mine the Narrows** | B-11 (revealed), laid by B-12, B-18, the F-16s (B-01, B-02), or requisitioned ferries (B-32, RI-11) | The strongest method. On success, add the ongoing effect "mines in the Narrows" (−1 to any shipping COA) until R-34 or another clearing COA removes it. Mining an international strait costs Opinion +1 (toward Cosmopolis). Strong REDFOR Cons: P-8 and MQ-9 watching the Narrows, and minelayers caught in the open. |
| **Declared exclusion zone** backed by coastal missiles and patrol boats | CB-1 (B-08), CB-2 (B-09, hidden), B-12 | A declaration alone is free and changes nothing. Backed by a credible threat, it is a COA. It is as strong as the batteries behind it: if REDFOR has struck CB-1, that is a strong Con. Naming CB-2 reveals it. |
| **Militia swarm and boardings** | B-18 | Repeats the *Fortune's Promise* seizure. Credible, and cheap to repeat. Seizing neutral ships moves Opinion +1. The boats are vulnerable to A-10s and helicopters. |
| **A demonstration strike** on a ship | CB-1, CB-2, F-16s with Harpoon | It works on insurers, but hitting a neutral ship is Opinion +1 and a good moment for I-02. Hitting a Cosmopolis-flagged or military ship is ordinary war. |
| **Hire Tekoan pirates** | Money (BI-15) | A COA aimed at a white-cell actor. Deniable at first. Tekoa takes the money but doesn't want combat in Z4 or Z5. Success raises the risk of I-08 for everyone. |
| **Scare the insurers** (information) | B-20 | Non-kinetic. Suggested: at most a Partial. It keeps the Strait Restricted with an ongoing −1 to shipping COAs, but doesn't close it on its own. A good Pro for a later closing COA. |

| **Blockships** (sinking ferries in the channel) | B-32 | A 25-mile channel can't be blocked with hulls. Rule it impossible, or scope it down to closing Fortune Harbor. |

**Assessing the outcome:**

- **Success:** Strait Traffic moves to **Closed**. Name the ongoing effect that keeps it closed (mines, an exclusion zone, a swarm) so REDFOR knows what it must remove.
- **Partial:** it stays **Restricted**, with an ongoing −1 to REDFOR shipping COAs (the worked example's "exclusion zone in the Narrows").
- **Exceptional:** Closed, plus a bonus: for example, insurers pull out and the closure is harder to reverse (−1 to REDFOR's first reopening COA).
- **Critical failure:** a neutral ship is hit or seized, Opinion +1, and consider I-02. Minelayers may be lost (Degrade B-12 or B-18).
- **Against a REDFOR convoy or escort in the same turn:** **directly opposed** (§4.1). Opposed roll; a tie leaves it Restricted.
- **Reopening:** each successful REDFOR clearing, escort, or strike COA moves the track one step toward Open, and removes the ongoing effect it targets. Mines need R-34 (N+2) or a strong argument.
- **Costs to Omak:** a long closure hurts Omak too (BI-13: port revenue is down). After two or three Closed turns, Stability pressure is fair. Hold teams to the Will clock in §5.

### 2.7 Unifying the NDF and PFA (S-B1)

S-B1 (A House Divided) gives **−1 to any BLUEFOR COA that needs NDF and PFA forces together** (check the owner column). BI-11 tells BLUEFOR the friction is real: the PFA is commandeering fuel trucks, and NDF air defense won't go under PFA command. There are two ways to remove S-B1: a successful **unified command COA**, which takes S-B1's −1 itself and removes it from the next turn on, or **Regime Stability reaching 5**.

**Ways they might try it:**

| Method | How to rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Joint headquarters or a unified command decree** by the President (his letter already demands it) | The standard path. A normal COA with S-B1's −1. Strong Pros: BI-11 (they know the problem), the President's direction, a common threat. Strong Cons: the NDF is apolitical and won't serve under the PFA (BI-11); its red lines (no internal repression) stand. Arguments that solve the actual friction (fuel allocation, a separate NDF air defense chain) deserve more credit than a general appeal for unity. |
| **Rally round the flag** to push Stability from 4 to 5 | An information COA (B-20) or a successful defense. One step reaches **5** and removes S-B1 ("national unity"). It's a real shortcut, so expect it. A REDFOR loyalist plot that ends in Critical failure (§1.5) can hand BLUEFOR the same step. |
| **Liaison and deconfliction** (liaison officers, agreed fuel shares, separate zones) | Counts as a unified command COA if it addresses BI-11's real friction. If it fixes only one thing (e.g. fuel), scope it down: an ongoing +1 to COAs that depend on that one thing, while S-B1 stays. |
| **Put the PFA militia under NDF command** | Plausible, and it answers BI-11. The cost is political for the Indigo government: on a Partial, apply Stability −1. |
| **Purge the NDF and install PFA loyalists** | It backfires. The NDF is apolitical and Stability depends on it. Suggested: it can't remove S-B1, it costs Stability −1, and it gives REDFOR a strong Pro for loyalist COAs. |
| **Just accept the −1** | Many teams will. Keep NDF-only and PFA-only COAs free of it. |

**Assessing the outcome:**

- **Success:** remove the S-B1 cue card **from the next turn**. Reorganizing takes time.
- **Partial:** removed, but one turn later than a Success.
- **Failure:** no change. **Critical failure:** the friction goes public. Stability −1, and REDFOR gets an established fact to use.
- **Once removed, S-B1 stays removed.** A REDFOR wedge COA (an info op stoking NDF–PFA distrust, S-B5 −1 while Stability ≥ 3) can't bring the card back. On success it creates an **ongoing effect** of −1 to joint COAs instead, until BLUEFOR removes it.
- **Interactions:** a REDFOR wedge COA in the same turn is **directly opposed** to the unified command COA. Opposed roll; a tie leaves S-B1 in place.

## 5. Balance watch

Three things can decide a game early. None of them breaks the rules, but the white cell should see them coming and narrate their costs honestly.

1. **The Closed-Strait clock.** Will −1 for each turn the Strait ends Closed, so BLUEFOR closing it on Turn 1 and holding it puts Will at **0 by the end of Turn 3**: a forced ceasefire. Closing it costs Omak too. Use BI-13 (port revenue down) to justify Stability pressure if the closure drags on, apply Opinion +1 for any neutral ship hit, and let a REDFOR victory move Will up where the narrative supports it.
2. **B-42 in Phase I.** Opinion starts at −1, so B-42's condition is met before the war starts. A Phase I request arrives Turn 4 and fails R3 for the rest of the game. Make sure the request is really for Asotin *crews*, not arms (B-40). RI-15 hints at it, so a REDFOR team that reads its intel has Turns 1–3 to try diplomacy or, with R-39 authority, interdiction in transit.
3. **B3 is one step away.** Opinion −1 → −2 achieves B3, so one good BLUEFOR information COA can settle it. REDFOR can pull it back, and B3 is scored at the end, so it rarely stays settled. Rule the opposing information COAs as an opposed roll.

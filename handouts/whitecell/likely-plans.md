**WHITE CELL ONLY.** It names both sides' hidden assets and secret objectives. *Fictional training scenario.*

**BLUF.** Expect REDFOR to open with air strikes to break Omak's coastal defenses and then push shipping through the Narrows. Expect BLUEFOR to close the Narrows, hide, and, at some point, hit Camas with PrSM. Both sides also have strong non-kinetic plays, and the best endings come from talks. This guide gives you the likely COAs before the game so you can rule on them fast. It is a forecast, not a script: reward whatever the teams actually argue well.

**How to use it.** Read it with the Handbook (§5 adjudication, §6 conditions) and the intelligence product key (Intelligence and Injects §3). During play, the tables in §4 are the quick reference. Watch §5 all afternoon.

**What drives them:** R1 (Strait **Open**), R2 (Stability **0**, or talks on Omak's government), R3 (no Asotin crews in Omak, and at least one shipment stopped). S-R1 gives them **3 turns** of high tempo before Camas strikes take −1, so they will want to act early.

## 2. BLUEFOR (Omak)

**What drives them:** B1 (hold Omak City, Stability ≥ 2), B2 (hold the coast, no Cosmopolis troops, sovereignty recognized in any deal), B3 (Opinion ≤ −2, or recognition by two states). Their intel overstates the invasion threat, so many teams will start defensive.

| RFI | Has Latah agreed to host bombers? | "Transports only. No bombers or fighters observed." |

**Phase I posture.** Expect BLUEFOR to brief relocating PAT-1, to place the NASAMS batteries (B-06a–c) in Z10–Z12, and maybe a secret COA: decoy SAM sites, or a minefield trap ("If Cosmopolis ships enter Z8, ...").

Civilian harm on Camas moves Opinion +1 (toward Cosmopolis). Consider I-09 if B-21 joins in.

## 3. Phase III and the endgame

By Debrief 1, most tables will be in one of three places. Expect the Phase III FRAGOs to follow.

| Situation after Turn 4 | REDFOR likely FRAGO | BLUEFOR likely FRAGO | White cell lever |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strait Closed, Will falling** | Clear and escort (R-34, R-36), or seek a deal before Will hits 0 | Hold the Strait Closed and run out the clock | I-12 mediation offer; I-04 legislature hearing |
| **REDFOR has air superiority, coast battered** | Escort convoys to reach Open; ask for R-37 | Survive: disperse, mobilize, push Opinion and recognition | I-03 if strikes risked civilians |
| **Stalemate** | Talks on the government (R2 Partial) and a Strait deal (R1) | A sovereignty deal (B2) and recognition (B3) | I-12. This is the game working. |

**Deals both sides can live with.** A reopened Strait that recognizes Omak's sovereignty achieves **R1 and B2** together. Formal talks on Omak's government give REDFOR **R2 Partial** at little cost to B1. Releasing the ship's crew is cheap goodwill for either side. If the teams start talking, give them the parley time.

## 5. Balance watch

Three things can decide a game early. None of them breaks the rules, but the white cell should see them coming and narrate their costs honestly.

1. **The Closed-Strait clock.** Will −1 for each turn the Strait ends Closed, so BLUEFOR closing it on Turn 1 and holding it puts Will at **0 by the end of Turn 3**: a forced ceasefire. Closing it costs Omak too. Use BI-13 (port revenue down) to justify Stability pressure if the closure drags on, apply Opinion +1 for any neutral ship hit, and let a REDFOR victory move Will up where the narrative supports it.
2. **B-42 in Phase I.** Opinion starts at −1, so B-42's condition is met before the war starts. A Phase I request arrives Turn 4 and fails R3 for the rest of the game. Make sure the request is really for Asotin *crews*, not arms (B-40). Consider announcing the deployment publicly when you approve it (RI-15 already hints at it), so REDFOR has three turns to try diplomacy or, with R-39 authority, interdiction in transit.
3. **B3 is one step away.** Opinion −1 → −2 achieves B3, so one good BLUEFOR information COA can settle it. REDFOR can pull it back, and B3 is scored at the end, so it rarely stays settled. Rule the opposing information COAs as an opposed roll.

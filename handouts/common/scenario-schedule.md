**You are expected to arrive at the LLAB having read, and knowing, everything in your read-ahead:** the Player Rules Handbook, the Scenario Brief, this Scenario Schedule, the Player Forms, and your team's documents. Your team's documents are secret, so don't show them to the other team.

*All countries, people, and events in this scenario are fictional. All military systems are real U.S. systems, described with unclassified, approximate capabilities.*

Each turn is about 3 days, so 7 turns cover about 3 weeks of conflict. The white cell keeps timelines realistic: moving forces 8,000 miles takes turns, not minutes (Scenario Brief §7).

### 1.3 Phase I – Planning

- You receive your intelligence products and team documents at least 24 hours before the LLAB so you can read them in advance. During Phase I (18 minutes), decide how your team is organized, synthesize your intelligence, build a plan, and file requests to posture your forces before the war starts.
- **Requests:** up to **4 in total** (RFIs and RFFs combined), **posted in your team's secret Slack channel by minute 12 (1357)** so the white cell can answer them while loggers hear the Phase I briefs. The white cell posts answers and rulings in threads under your requests after the kickoff inject, before Turn 1. Phase I RFFs follow normal arrival timing (Scenario Brief §7), counted from Turn 1.
- **The brief.** In the last 3 minutes (minutes 15–18), give an **oral brief** of your plan to your own logger, organized like a **five-paragraph OPORD**: Situation, Mission, Execution, Sustainment, Command & Signal. Use the template in the Player Forms. Nothing written is required.
- **Planned COAs.** The brief may name **up to 3 planned COAs** in order for Phase II, e.g. "Turn 1: X; Turn 2: Y; Turn 3: Z." The logger writes them down. When you later execute a planned COA *substantially as briefed, and the situation still supports it*, you get **+1 (Planned)**. If events make it invalid (the target is gone, you lost the assets, or the enemy forced a reaction), the bonus is lost. Recognizing that and adapting is the point.
- **Organize your team.** Only your flight commander is assigned. The team decides everything else, for example who runs operations, intel, logistics, and requests, and who briefs COAs and Cons. Rotate briefers so everyone talks.

### 1.4 Phase III – Planning and FRAGO

- **Command handover and FRAGO.** After Debrief 1, the **Phase III–IV flight commander** takes command. The new flight commander gives a **1-minute oral Fragmentary Order (FRAGO)** to the team's logger: a brief situation update, updated Commander's Intent, and **up to 2 new planned COAs for Phase IV (Turns 5–7)**, which earn the same **+1 (Planned)** bonus when executed as briefed.

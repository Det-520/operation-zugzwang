**Audience:** every player, REDFOR and BLUEFOR, and the white cell. Nothing in this document is secret. It repeats Scenario Brief §6 (keep the two the same) and adds cue cards for the table. **Printing:** one copy per table, in colour if possible; cut the cue cards apart along the dashed lines and stand them face up beside the map.

Public, and on cue cards at the table. Use them in your Pros and Cons: each one that applies to a COA is worth **±1** until it is removed. The white cell writes the turn on a card when it is removed.

### S-R1 · The 8,000-Mile Tail

**Constraint on REDFOR.** Reinforcements arrive slowly. Camas can sustain high-tempo operations for **3 turns**. From **Turn 4**, Camas-based strike COAs take **−1** until resupplied.

**Removed by:** a successful resupply COA, or an RFF for a sustainment package.

**Removed on turn:** ______

### S-R2 · Single Hub

**Constraint on REDFOR.** All forward forces sit on Camas. A successful BLUEFOR strike on Camas affects **every** counter there, so one roll can Degrade several.

**Removed by:** ACE dispersal, a big project of **2 successful COAs** (e.g. Latah basing + an ACE package).

**Removed on turn:** ______

### S-R3 · Latah Hesitates

**Constraint on REDFOR.** Latah allows logistics only. **No combat sorties from Latah.**

**Removed by:** a successful diplomatic COA, or International Opinion reaches +3.

**Removed on turn:** ______

### S-R4 · Legitimacy Deficit

**Constraint on REDFOR.** International Opinion starts at −1. COAs that rely on **third-party cooperation** take **−1** while Opinion ≤ −1.

**Removed by:** raising International Opinion to 0 or more.

**Removed on turn:** ______

### S-B1 · A House Divided

**Constraint on BLUEFOR.** COAs that need NDF **and** PFA forces to work together take **−1**.

**Removed by:** a successful "unified command" COA, or Regime Stability reaches 5. The unified command COA takes this −1 itself, and the removal starts next turn, because reorganizing takes time.

**Removed on turn:** ______

### S-B2 · Sapphire Loyalists

**Constraint on BLUEFOR.** A small minority. REDFOR may argue for defections or sabotage, but such COAs take **−1** because of S-B5.

**Removed by:** a successful counterintelligence COA, which removes the option entirely.

**Removed on turn:** ______

### S-B3 · Home Ground

**Advantage for BLUEFOR.** **+1** to BLUEFOR **defensive** COAs in Omak zones (Z10–Z12): defending, dispersing, hiding, repairing, ambushing, counterintelligence. Defenders know the terrain, and any ground incursion is very costly.

**Permanent.**

### S-B4 · Asotin's Backing

**Advantage for BLUEFOR.** BLUEFOR requests for Asotin arms and intel are **routinely approved**. Bigger escalations need conditions. Supplies arrive via the West Approach and by air. REDFOR must argue a successful COA to interdict them, and interdiction risks Asotin escalation.

**Permanent.**

### S-B5 · Supportive Population

**Advantage for BLUEFOR.** Regime Stability starts at 4. REDFOR COAs that rely on **Omak civilians** (SOF operating in Omak, loyalist uprisings, information ops aimed at Omak's public) take **−1**. BLUEFOR RFIs about REDFOR activity *inside Omak* get full answers.

**Eroded:** if Regime Stability falls to 2 or lower, the −1 no longer applies. **On turn:** ______

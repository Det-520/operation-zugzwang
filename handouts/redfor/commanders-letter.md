COSMOPOLIS NATIONAL DEFENSE FORCES

12 November 2026

MEMORANDUM FOR COMMANDER, CNDF FORWARD FORCES, CAMAS AIR BASE

FROM: Chairman of the Joint Staff, Cosmopolis National Defense Forces

SUBJECT: Strategic Guidance for Operations in the Strait of Great Fortune

1\. The President has directed the CNDF to prepare to restore freedom of navigation through the Strait of Great Fortune. If the Government of Omak does not change course, expect an order to begin operations with little warning. This memorandum gives you my guidance until then. The President's objectives are at the attachment.

2\. Situation. Since the PFA took power in Omak three months ago, the new government has refused to honor the defense treaty and has announced a "review" of every agreement we hold with Omak, starting with our port access on the Strait. Asotin cargo aircraft now land in Omak every night. The President judges, and I agree, that a Strait controlled by a PFA government aligned with Asotin would put our trade at risk and call into question our commitments to every partner that relies on them.

3\. Forces and sustainment. Plan to fight with the forces already on Camas for the first several days. Everything else must cross 8,000 miles of ocean. At high tempo, Camas can sustain operations for about nine days before munitions and fuel become limiting. Submit requests for forces and for sustainment early, because lead times are long.

4\. Authorities. Without further approval you are not authorized to:

a. Put ground forces on Omak territory.

b. Strike leadership or government targets in Omak City, Omak's capital.

c. Engage Asotin forces or Asotin-flagged shipping.

d. Operate in Tekoan territory or airspace without the consent of Tekoan authorities.

If the situation requires any of these, send the request to me with your rationale. I will present it to the President.

These limits apply to military operations only. The President has authorized you to negotiate in the Government's name, with the Government of Omak directly or through intermediaries, and with Latah, Tekoa, Asotin, and other governments. You do not need further approval to open talks or to conclude an agreement. An agreement that commits forces to any act in subparagraphs 4a through 4d still requires that approval.

5\. Political considerations. The legislature is divided on this operation, and public support is narrow. It will not survive heavy casualties or a long campaign. The disclosure of our support to the Sapphire campaign has cost us credibility abroad, and our adversaries will publicize every civilian casualty. Avoid civilian harm, and consider how each action will be reported before you take it.

6\. Partners. Latah has granted logistics access only. Basing negotiations continue, but do not plan combat operations from Latah until they conclude. Tekoa has declared neutrality.

7\. Enemy. Most of the NDF's equipment was bought from us, and we know its capabilities and limitations in detail. The Joint Intelligence Center assesses that a significant part of the NDF officer corps remains sympathetic to the Sapphire government. If that is accurate, it may offer options short of force. Your intelligence estimate summarizes the supporting reporting.

8\. Intent. Achieve the President's objectives at a cost our people will accept. Keep the force intact, keep the conflict contained to Omak, and act in a way our partners can defend in public.

&nbsp;

BENJAMIN H. HAGGARTY, General, CNDF\
Chairman of the Joint Staff

# The Tongue and Quill: Summary for Operation Zugzwang

**Source:** AFH 33-337, *The Tongue and Quill*, 27 May 2015 (Change 1, 19 Nov 2015), `AFH_33-337_Tongue_and_Quill.pdf`. Page numbers below are the handbook's printed page numbers.

This summary covers what the project needs: the writing style for every handout, and the formats for the in-game documents written as Air Force correspondence. §7 says which handout uses which format. The intelligence product formats don't come from the Tongue and Quill, and §6 covers them separately.

---

## 1. The core rules (ch. 1–2, 7–8, pp. 3–13, 65–102)

**FOCUS** (p. 5). Every product should be:

- **Focused:** answer the question, the whole question, and nothing but the question.
- **Organized:** present information in a sequence the reader can follow.
- **Clear:** make each word count.
- **Understanding:** write for this audience and its expectations.
- **Supported:** back each point with logic and evidence.

**Seven steps** (p. 9): (1) analyze purpose and audience, (2) research, (3) support your ideas, (4) organize and outline, (5) draft, (6) edit, (7) get feedback and approval.

**Plain language is required** (p. 2). The Plain Writing Act of 2010 and DoDI 5025.13 apply to everything written in an official capacity.

**Drafting rules that matter most:**

- **Bottom line up front (BLUF)** (pp. 11, 67). State the purpose in the first paragraph. "Most Air Force readers don't have the time or patience to read a staff paper written like a mystery novel with a surprise ending."
- **One main point per paragraph**, led by a topic sentence (p. 69). Staff paragraphs are usually 3–7 sentences.
- **Active voice** (pp. 73–75). Put the actor before the verb. Passive voice is acceptable when the actor is unknown or unimportant, or in diplomatic writing where softer language is wanted.
- **Prefer short, spoken words** to long, bookish ones: *but* over *however*, *so* over *therefore*, *also* over *in addition* (p. 70).
- **Conclude** by restating the main point. Don't apologize or add new ideas at the end (pp. 67–68).
- **Edit top-down** (p. 93): content and organization first, then paragraphs and sentences, then spelling and punctuation.

**Writing for a senior official** (p. 162). Keep it simple ("Get to the point, make it and move on"). Go easy on modifiers: a senior official doesn't need to be *very* interested, just interested. Avoid emotional tones. Put details in attachments.

## 3. The official memorandum (ch. 14, pp. 163–190)

The standard format for official business inside and between government organizations. Use it for staff guidance, internal memos, threat assessments, and memorandums of conversation.

**Page setup:** 1-inch margins (wider for short memos), 12-point Times New Roman, letterhead or plain bond paper, black ink (pp. 163–164).

**Heading** (pp. 164–168):

| Element | Placement and format |
| --- | --- |
| Date | 1 inch from the right edge, 1.75 inches from the top |
| `MEMORANDUM FOR` | Second line below the date. Two spaces, then the addressee in UPPERCASE. Multiple addressees align under the first; long lists use `DISTRIBUTION`. |
| `ATTENTION:` / `THROUGH:` | Optional, on the line below the addressee |
| `FROM:` | Second line below `MEMORANDUM FOR`, flush left. Organization and office symbol. |
| `SUBJECT:` | Second line below `FROM:`. Title case. Wrapped lines align under the first word. |
| `References:` | Optional. One reference goes in parentheses after the subject; two or more get their own element. |

**Text** (p. 169):

- Begins on the second line below the subject (or references).
- Single-spaced, with a blank line between paragraphs.
- **Number every paragraph** (1., 2., then a., b.). A single paragraph is not numbered.
- The first line is flush left, never indented.
- Put contact information in the last paragraph.

**Closing** (pp. 169–173):

- **Authority line** (`FOR THE COMMANDER`), only when someone signs on a commander's behalf.
- **Signature block:** 5 lines below the text, 4.5 inches from the left edge. Line 1: NAME IN CAPS, grade, service ("Colonel" and general officer ranks are spelled out). Line 2: duty title. Never put the signature block alone on a page.
- **Attachments:** 3 lines below the signature block, flush left. `Attachment:` or `3 Attachments:`, each described specifically (never "as stated").
- **cc:** and **DISTRIBUTION:** follow the attachments.

**Variants** (p. 177; examples on pp. 178–190 are images in the PDF):

- **Indorsement** ("1st Ind"): a reply or comment added to the original memo, used for coordination and for recommendations up the chain. The BI-05 NDF comment uses it.
- **IN TURN:** routes one memo to several offices in sequence.
- **Memorandum for record (MFR):** an informal record of a conversation, decision, or event.
- **After-action report** and **trip report**.

## 4. The personal letter (ch. 15, pp. 191–218)

Used for correspondence from one person to another when a memorandum would be too impersonal: letters of welcome, appreciation, or condolence, and letters to civilians or senior officials outside the chain of command.

- **Date** in the same position as the memo.
- **Return address** flush left, 2 lines below the date. **Receiver's address** 3 lines below that.
- **Salutation:** "Dear" plus title or rank and last name, **no punctuation** (Air Force open punctuation). "Dear Mr. President", "Dear Madam Secretary" (p. 194).
- **Text:** don't number paragraphs. Indent the first line of each paragraph 0.5 inch. Put references in the first paragraph instead of a references element.
- **Close:** "Sincerely", no punctuation, 4.5 inches from the left edge. Signature block 5 lines below. No authority line.

## 5. Air Force "papers" (ch. 16, pp. 219–228)

The templates are images in the PDF (pp. 222–228). Use them if a future handout needs a staff paper.

| Paper | Use | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Point paper** | Memory jogger on a single issue | 1 page, bullets |
| **Talking paper** | Speaking notes for a planned briefing | 1 page, bullets, key reference data |
| **Bullet background paper** | Background on an issue, or a summary of a staff package | 1+ pages, bullet statements |
| **Background paper** | Detailed background for a decision maker | Multi-page, full sentences, numbered paragraphs |
| **Position paper** | Proposes, evaluates, or advocates a position | Multi-page, full sentences, numbered paragraphs |

Bullets must be **accurate, brief, and specific (ABS)**, with one idea each (p. 220).

## 6. Intelligence products (not in the Tongue and Quill)

The Tongue and Quill doesn't cover intelligence reporting. The RI-xx and BI-xx products follow general, unclassified conventions of U.S. and NATO intelligence writing instead:

- **Raw reporting** (Intelligence Information Reports, source reports) passes on what a source said, without judgment, and carries the caveat "THIS IS AN INFORMATION REPORT, NOT FINALLY EVALUATED INTELLIGENCE." It has a header block (serial, country, subject, date of information, source description), a short summary, numbered paragraphs of text, and collector comments kept apart from the text.
- **Analytic products** (assessments, intelligence notes) lead with the bottom line and use **estimative language**. The likelihood words and the confidence levels follow ICD 203, *Analytic Standards*. The word table is in each side's `intel/00-read-me-first.md`.
- **Source and information ratings** use the NATO "Admiralty" system (a letter for source reliability, a number for information credibility).
- **Message formats** (diplomatic cables, MISREPs) are written in capitals with numbered paragraphs, and the drafts reproduce that layout in code blocks.

These describe the general shape of real formats, not any exact current template. The in-world markings ("COSMOPOLIS SECRET") are fictional.
